The curriculum

You were taught to solve mathematics. We teach you to read it.

Reading, then writing, then thinking, in the language mathematics is written in. Whether you are racing ahead of your class or held up by an idea that will not settle, the way through is the same. There are a few ways to begin. The right one depends on where you are now.

Where you stand

The Portrait

Before you choose anything, it helps to know exactly where you stand. Over two short sittings you work through questions built to show how you read mathematics, how you set your reasoning down, and what you do when you are stuck. Not how many marks you drop. You come away with a written account of how you work and a clear recommendation for what comes next.

The Prologue

If you are beginning Higher Level mathematics in the IB Diploma, some of its hardest ideas are already waiting for you. Proof, complex numbers and vectors. The Prologue takes you through them in a small group, before your class arrives, so you meet them for the second time when it matters most.

One to one

Read, then write, then think.

This is the heart of what we do. Taught one to one, it begins with learning to read mathematics properly. We take the definitions, notation and proofs you have only half met and stay with each until it genuinely makes sense to you. From there you learn to write it and then to think in it, on a question of your own.

The Practice Room

When you stop speaking a language, you begin to forget it. Mathematics is no different. The Practice Room keeps the conversation going, with a set of problems each Monday and a written commentary each Saturday about what the week was really asking. Nothing new to learn and nothing to catch up on.
