Maths you do not touch is maths you lose.

One good problem a day. Ten minutes. That is the whole idea.

Think about when you actually do mathematics. Something is due, so you work at it. Nothing is due, so you do not. Weeks go by. Then you sit down again and it feels stiff, like something you used to be good at.

That is not you losing your ability. It is what happens to any skill you leave alone. The Practice Room is the smallest possible fix. One good problem every weekday morning, ten minutes, often less.

On Saturday I write about the week. Not the answers, but how to see them. What the question was really asking. The first thing you would try that does not work. The move that does.

Do that for a term and you never have to get back into mathematics. You never left.
